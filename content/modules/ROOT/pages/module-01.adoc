## Step 1: About This Lab

== Objective

By the end of this guided lab exercise, you will be able to:

* Enumerate outstanding CVEs and security errata on RHEL 10 and apply security patches.
* Run an OpenSCAP audit against a CIS benchmark and interpret the compliance report.
* Automatically remediate a system to *CIS Level 1 (Server)* and verify the result.
* Explain the difference between *standard* hardening (Level 1) and *maximum* lockdown (Level 2).
* Create operational rule exceptions with `autotailor` without editing vendor content.
* Bake a compliance profile into an OS image blueprint with *Red Hat Image Builder*.

== Introduction

Security compliance is how enterprises prove — to auditors, to customers, and to themselves —
that their systems are configured safely. Doing it by hand across a fleet is impossible; doing
it _automatically_ and _repeatably_ is the job. Red Hat Enterprise Linux 10 ships the tooling
to measure a system against an industry benchmark, fix what fails, manage the exceptions every
real organization needs, and prevent *security drift* by building compliance into images from
day one.

In this lab you play the newest administrator at *Super-Business*, a company whose middle
manager, *Manager Bill*, has strong opinions about security and a weak memory of how any of it
works. Luckily, *Sysadmin Grace* — who has been cleaning up after Bill for years — is around
to point you in the right direction.

[quote, Manager Bill]
____
"Security is simple, people. I read a blog once. Just turn on _all_ the security and we're
compliant. Make it happen by end of day."
____

[quote, Sysadmin Grace]
____
"...and _that_ is why we measure first, fix with automation, document our exceptions, and bake
it into the image. Ignore the 'all the security' part. Follow me."
____

=== Lab Modules

. *About This Lab* — You are here: compliance, the toolchain, and the environment.
. *Vulnerability & Patch Management* — Enumerate CVEs/errata with `dnf updateinfo`; apply security patches.
. *Baseline Compliance Audit* — Scan an out-of-the-box system against *CIS Level 1* with OpenSCAP.
. *Automated Remediation* — Remediate to CIS Level 1 and re-scan to prove it worked.
. *Standard vs. Maximum* — Audit *CIS Level 2* on `node1` and contrast it with Level 1.
. *Operational Exceptions* — Use `autotailor` to waive a rule without touching vendor files.
. *Compliance-as-Code* — Bake CIS Level 1 into an image blueprint with Red Hat Image Builder.

== Confirm the toolchain

First, confirm you are on RHEL 10 with the OpenSCAP scanner installed:

[source,bash,role=execute]
----
oscap --version
----

Now list the CIS profiles shipped in the RHEL 10 SCAP Security Guide content:

[source,bash,role=execute]
----
oscap info --profiles /usr/share/xml/scap/ssg/content/ssg-rhel10-ds.xml | grep -i cis
----

You should see several, including `...cis_server_l1` (Level 1 Server) and `...cis` (Level 2
Server). These profile IDs are what you will scan against throughout the lab.

When you are ready, continue to the next module to start measuring.
